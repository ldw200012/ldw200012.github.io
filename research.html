Projects

Research

I study how marine robots can localize and map when cameras fade, GPS disappears, and inertial sensors slowly wander. After several years on LiDAR perception, my current focus is SLAM from 2.5D bathymetry, together with geomagnetic mapping and GNSS-denied surface navigation.

Underwater SLAM and bathymetry

Current work focuses on SLAM from 2.5D bathymetric structure. Seafloor relief is a stable spatial cue for AUVs, and I am interested in representations that stay consistent under sonar noise, sparse coverage, and long-duration drift.

Geomagnetic mapping for localization

Earth’s magnetic field varies smoothly underwater and can serve as a map when visual landmarks are scarce. I have worked on Gaussian process models of geomagnetic intensity that remain useful even when the vehicle’s inertial navigation is drifting, including spatiotemporal mapping for AUV localization.

Marine LiDAR perception

Harbors and waterfronts are cluttered, reflective, and only partly observed. I develop LiDAR methods for marine vessel re-identification, including a branch-augmented network presented at OCEANS 2025, and CALM-Net, shown as a late-breaking poster at IROS 2025 and now under review at IEEE Sensors Journal.

GNSS-denied marine navigation

When satellite positioning drops out at sea, surface vehicles still have to navigate and track targets. I contribute to radar-based USV navigation and to collaborative USV–UAV search, including Team KAIST’s MBZIRC 2023 system. Both lines of work are under review at Robotics and Autonomous Systems.

Perception for autonomous surface vessels

Together with colleagues at MORIN, I surveyed maritime perception datasets and sensing technologies for ASVs. The goal is a clearer picture of what current sensors and benchmarks actually cover, and where they fail at sea.
